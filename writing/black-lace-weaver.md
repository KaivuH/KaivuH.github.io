# The Black Lace-Weaver Spider

They knew their mother through her tight embrace with death,
through her slow, heaving breaths and closed eyes,
through her tender brushes as she labored nearby as they were born.

There were only three things in their worlds then:
their mother,
the nutrient-rich eggs she laid
for them to eat,
and each other,
their innumerable bodies sliding
past each other and around their little world.

They knew they loved her.
They knew too that they’d forget her soon.
That was the nature of their short lives.

As they grew, molted,
she began to sing the old song to them.
It boiled their blue
blood,
steeled their senses,
sharpened their venom.

As the song grew deeper and her drumming more insistent,
they began to flock to her.
They knew that she wanted them nearby as she died, so she could
give them her most precious gift,
That which she’d been saving her whole life.

Her song had taken a note of panic.
A few leapt to her back,
She tried to shake them off,

And her struggle became the song,
They poured onto her,
Caressing, rending,

They nuzzled into her as she breathed her last.
